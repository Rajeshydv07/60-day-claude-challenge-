# Day 6 - ATS Resume Optimization

This is Day 6 of my 60-Day AI Challenge. Today I learned about how Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) scan resumes and how to optimize my resume using Claude.

What is ATS Optimization?

When we apply for jobs, a software (ATS) usually scans our resume before a human recruiter even sees it. If the formatting is too complex or if key details are missing, the system might reject the resume automatically. Today I learned how to format and structure my resume so that it passes these scanners and scores higher.

My Resume Before vs After

Before this, I had a resume that used multiple columns. I also had a generic Objective section that focused on what I wanted (like getting selected for the Amazon ML Summer School) rather than what I can do. My project descriptions were mostly descriptive and did not have many metrics to show the impact.

Here is what I changed:
* Layout: I changed it from a multi-column format to a clean, single-column format. Multi-columns confuse ATS parsers, but a single column reads linearly and safely.
* Professional Summary: I replaced the Objective with a Professional Summary. I highlighted my B.Tech IT background (2027), Python, Java, ReactJS, and Scikit-learn skills.
* Standard Headings: I used standard headings in all-caps like EDUCATION, TECHNICAL SKILLS, and PROJECTS so the parser knows exactly where to look.
* Quantified Impact: I put important numbers at the beginning of project bullets. For example, I mentioned the 88% accuracy of the Random Forest model and the 25% noise reduction in the preprocessing phase.
* Skills Mapping: I grouped my skills into clear labels like Languages, Frameworks & Libraries, and AI/ML. I also added Prompt Engineering and RAG basics directly into my technical skills section.

The Score Improvement

I ran my resume through the scanner and got a score before and after the changes.

My score went from 54/100 to 87/100, which is a jump of 33 points.

Here is how the scores changed by category:
* Formatting & structure: went from 40 to 90
* Keyword density: went from 55 to 85
* Section headings: went from 50 to 95
* Quantified impact: went from 60 to 88
* Summary / objective: went from 0 to 85
* Skills mapping: went from 65 to 90

Key Learnings

* Single column layouts are much safer for ATS parsers than multi-column ones.
* Section headers should be standard (like EDUCATION, TECHNICAL SKILLS, PROJECTS) instead of custom names.
* Put metrics and results early in your bullet points to show impact.
* Grouping skills into categories helps ATS filters index your profile correctly.
